# Source Specification

## Definition

The Source Specification is the canonical expression of expected behavior, business rules, constraints, assumptions, edge cases, error conditions, and acceptance conditions for a proposed change.

## Purpose

Converts business intent into an execution-independent behavioral contract.

## Problem Solved

Feature requests and user stories rarely contain enough precision for reliable execution without additional interpretation.

## Inputs

- Relevant prior concepts, context, evidence, or decisions.

## Outputs

- A governed representation, decision, assessment, or improvement.

## Dependencies

- See related canonical concepts.

## Consumers

- Downstream transformations and governance decisions.

## Quality Criteria

- Clear
- Traceable
- Proportionate to risk
- Technology-independent
- Consistent with the North Star

## Common Mistakes

- Treating the concept as a checklist without understanding its purpose.
- Embedding implementation-specific assumptions in the canonical definition.
- Hiding uncertainty or deviations.
