# IDE-BoK Constitution

**Status:** Foundational  
**Version:** 0.1

## Preamble

The Intent-Driven Engineering Body of Knowledge exists to define, preserve, and evolve the principles, concepts, and reference models of Intent-Driven Software Engineering.

Its purpose is not to prescribe technologies, methodologies, vendors, organizational structures, or implementation tools.

> Preserve business intent through controlled transformations until it becomes validated operational behavior.

## Article I — Scope

The IDE-BoK defines engineering concepts, principles, transformation models, governance models, quality models, reference artifacts, and terminology.

It does not canonically define programming languages, AI vendors, software frameworks, project management methodologies, organizational hierarchies, or implementation technologies.

## Article II — North Star

> Preserve business intent while continuously reducing uncertainty across every engineering transformation.

A concept that does not reinforce this objective does not belong in the authoritative Body of Knowledge.

## Article III — First Principles

1. **Intent precedes implementation.**
2. **Context enables correct execution.**
3. **Every transformation introduces uncertainty.**
4. **Every transformation must preserve intent.**
5. **Engineering exists wherever meaning can be lost.**
6. **Readiness is more valuable than premature execution.**
7. **Validation is continuous.**
8. **Evidence is the final authority of engineering quality.**
9. **Autonomy is proportional to uncertainty.**
10. **Learning continuously improves the engineering system.**

## Article IV — Architectural Boundary

The IDE-BoK governs everything required to transform business intent into an execution-ready state, the boundary required to delegate execution safely, and everything required to evaluate outcomes and improve the engineering system.

Execution technologies are external capabilities and may evolve independently.

## Article V — Concept Model

Every concept shall exist as a first-class knowledge entity with:

- Definition
- Purpose
- Problem Solved
- Inputs
- Outputs
- Dependencies
- Consumers
- Quality Criteria
- Related Concepts
- Common Mistakes

## Article VI — Knowledge Classification

### Foundational Knowledge
Enduring principles and concepts. Changes rarely.

### Core Knowledge
Principal domains and relationships. Changes deliberately.

### Operational Knowledge
Templates, checklists, examples, cards, and playbooks. Changes frequently.

### Experimental Knowledge
Hypotheses and unvalidated proposals. Cannot modify canonical knowledge until promoted.

## Article VII — Evolution Rules

A concept may enter the IDE-BoK only when it solves a distinct problem, avoids duplication, strengthens coherence, preserves simplicity, reinforces the North Star, demonstrates practical applicability, and has sufficient evidence for its classification.

## Article VIII — Simplicity

A smaller number of coherent concepts is preferable to a large collection of overlapping ideas.

## Article IX — Technology Independence

Canonical definitions shall remain independent from AI models, vendors, orchestration platforms, workflow tools, cloud providers, languages, and frameworks.

## Article X — Evidence

Foundational and Core Knowledge shall evolve through practical adoption, repeatable outcomes, measurable improvements, validated experiments, observed failure modes, architectural consistency, and review. Opinion alone is insufficient.

## Article XI — Visual First

Every major concept should be explainable through a simple visual model. Visual communication is a first-class engineering artifact.

## Article XII — Single Source of Truth

Every concept has one authoritative definition. Books, skills, playbooks, infographics, cards, training, presentations, and websites derive from it and do not redefine it.

## Article XIII — Layered Knowledge

1. Mental Models
2. Visual Frameworks
3. Reference Concepts
4. Detailed Specifications
5. Reference Implementations

## Article XIV — Continuous Evolution

Foundational Knowledge evolves cautiously. Core Knowledge evolves deliberately. Operational Knowledge evolves frequently. Experimental Knowledge evolves freely.

## Article XV — Integrity Test

Every material change must answer:

1. Does it preserve the North Star?
2. Does it reduce uncertainty?
3. Does it simplify or clarify the model?
4. Does it improve internal coherence?

## Closing Statement

The IDE-BoK does not standardize how software is built. It preserves the principles that ensure business intent survives every transformation until it becomes validated operational behavior.
